## Top 10 likely VC questions

**1. "Isn't this just a wrapper on Hunyuan3D / TRELLIS / Meshy?"**
The models make a mesh; Vi3W owns the workflow around it: sketch input, in-browser editing, export, and (Q1 '27) team review. The pipeline is model-agnostic, so each better open model improves Vi3W instead of replacing it. The data asset is sketch-to-model pairs that text-to-3D tools never collect.

**2. "Why won't Meshy or Adobe just add sketch input?"**
They might. Sketch input alone is not the moat. The moat is where the team's projects, versions and approvals live. Single-user generators are built around one creator and a credit wallet. Vi3W is built around a shared link that pulls in the whole team. Speed matters too: one founder shipped the live product in about a year.

**3. "You're a solo founder. Who builds this with you?"**
The first two hires are funded by this round: a 3D/ML engineer and a full-stack engineer. [Name any candidates you're already talking to.] I built the whole pipeline and ran paid 3D work for studios in Europe and China, so I can hire and judge both roles.

**4. "400 users from 3M views is 0.01% conversion. Why so low?"**
The reels went viral with a general audience, not only game developers. Pilot access was gated and needed manual onboarding. The stronger signal is that 30% of pilot users come back every week. The November public launch removes the gate and reaches game-dev channels directly.

**5. "What are your unit economics? Can a $19 user cost you $100?"**
Compute is $0.08–$1 per generation. Pilot users average about 5 generations a month, about $1.50 in compute against a $19 plan, roughly 90% gross margin. Credits cap the worst case, and the expensive steps move to self-hosted open models as volume grows.

**6. "Why games first, and why small teams?"**
Small teams have the pain (no 3D artist), short buying cycles (a card, not procurement) and gather in a few communities where Vi3W already has reach. About 150K active Play Store game developers and 19K Steam releases a year make a clear, reachable segment.

**7. "How does this become venture-scale? $520M TAM isn't huge."**
Games are the entry point. The same assets, made simulation-ready, feed CAD and robotics synthetic data, a much larger market. Each step reuses the pipeline and the data. The seed milestone is the first CAD or robotics pilot.

**8. "What does the $500K buy, and what does the company look like after?"**
About 20 months of runway: 45% product and engineering (2 hires), 20% compute, 20% growth, 15% operations. The milestones are $25K MRR, 600+ paying teams, team collaboration live and a first CAD/robotics pilot. That is a seed-ready company.
